- **business_impact**: Direct elongation of pre-lit cycle time and cash conversion; missing records at demand stage weaken valuation leverage (CasePeer notes delays and importance of early requests: https://www.casepeer.com/blog/medical-records-management/).

- **current_process**: When treatment stabilizes or enough records are gathered, staff assemble a demand package: demand letter, police report, photos/video, medical records/bills, wage loss, pain and suffering narrative, future care (CASEpeer guide enumerates standard contents and workflow; insurer response deadlines often 1 week to 1 month: https://www.casepeer.com/blog/how-to-prepare-a-personal-injury-demand-package/). Vendors and software provide drafting aids/templates (CloudLex overview: https://www.cloudlex.com/law-firm-management/what-settlement-demand-package/; EvenUp guidance on demand components: https://www.evenuplaw.com/blog/winning-demand-package/). Handoff: Attorney reviews and approves; package is sent to the insurer/TPA via email, portal upload, or mail.

- **why_painful**: Demand quality sets the negotiation anchor. Human-intensive summarization and narrative writing consume scarce paralegal/attorney time and are highly sensitive to record completeness (CASEpeer/CloudLex/EvenUp resources on components and timing).
- **business_impact**: Longer drafting cycles and rework delay first offers. Poorly supported demands lower opening offers and extend negotiation rounds, pushing fee realization out.
### Item 6
- **stage**: Negotiation with insurer
- **owner_roles**: ['Pre-Litigation Attorney/Negotiator', 'Demand Writer/Paralegal (supports counteroffers)', 'Client (consent on offers)']
- **current_process**: After submission, carriers typically respond in 1–4 weeks with accept/deny/counter (CASEpeer notes 1 week to 1 month typical review windows: https://www.casepeer.com/blog/how-to-prepare-a-personal-injury-demand-package/). Negotiations are iterative: adjusters start low, counsel counters with facts/medical proof until agreement or suit is filed (Nolo on negotiation cadence and low-first-offer pattern: https://www.nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/how-the-negotiation-process-works-injury-case.html; Justia overview echoes iterative pattern: https://www.justia.com/injury/settlement-negotiations-in-personal-injury-cases/). Many carriers use software like Colossus or similar systems to evaluate BI claims, influencing offers and requiring targeted documentation (Nolo on Colossus: https://www.nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/how-the-colossus-computer-program-estimates-accident-settlement-values.html; Consumer Federation of America report on insurer computer claims practices: https://consumerfed.org/pdfs/Studies.ComputerClaims06-04-12.pdf). Handoff: Once client consents to settle, matter moves to lien resolution and disbursement.
- **common_tools**: ['Phone/email; occasional insurer portals', 'CMS tasking and offer tracking', 'Valuation worksheets or internal calculators']
- **manual_steps**: ['Phone/email negotiation; documenting each offer/counter', 'Gathering supplemental proof (additional bills, provider letters, photos)', 'Client counseling and consent documentation']
- **pain_points**: ['Multiple negotiation rounds prolong cycle time; Attorney must craft carrier-specific arguments that match software-driven valuation (Colossus).', 'Adjuster turnover or reassignment resets progress; staff re-send packages.', 'Client expectation management requires repeated consultations.']
- **why_painful**: Negotiation hinges on human persuasion and tailored evidence. Carrier software constraints and staffing changes generate rework and delay.
- **business_impact**: Longer WIP carry; attorney time not scalable across a high-volume docket; risk of litigation filing if stalemated, increasing costs.
### Item 7
- **stage**: Settlement disbursement (lien resolution, trust accounting, client payment)
- **owner_roles**: ['Lien Resolution Specialist', 'Settlement Coordinator', 'Attorney (approvals/negotiations)', 'Accounting Manager/Bookkeeper']
- **current_process**: Post-agreement, firm resolves statutory and contractual liens (Medicare, Medicaid, provider liens) and performs trust accounting/distribution. Medicare: use the MSPRP to manage conditional payments; the Final Conditional Payment process allows time/date-stamped summaries pre-settlement and disputes are addressed within 11 business days (CMS MSPRP: https://www.cms.gov/medicare/coordination-benefits-recovery/overview/secondary-payer-recovery-portal; Final CP process: https://www.cms.gov/Medicare/Coordination-of-Benefits-and-Recovery/Coordination-of-Benefits-and-Recovery-Overview/MSPRP/Downloads/Final-Conditional-Payment-Process.pdf; demand calculation/11 business days note: https://gov-mirror.org/www.cms.gov/medicare/coordination-benefits-recovery/beneficiary-services/demand-calculation-options). After settlement, CMS issues a demand and interest accrues if unpaid per CMS rules (interest accrual from date of demand and assessed each 30-day period if unresolved: https://www.cms.gov/medicare/coordination-benefits-recovery/beneficiary-services/recovery-process; Synergy notes typical 7–14 days to final demand and 60-day repayment window before interest accrues: https://partnerwithsynergy.com/liens/liens-we-handle/medicare/faq/). Medicaid lien processes are state-specific and often will not issue final lien amounts until settlement info is provided (Epiq primer and vendor commentary: https://www.epiqglobal.com/en-us/resource-center/articles/lien-profile-a-short-primer-on-medicaid-liens; Massive on Medi-Cal process change requiring settlement info: https://gomassive.com/california-medicaid-health-care-liens/). Trust/IOLTA: firms must perform monthly three-way reconciliations (bank statement, trust ledger, client ledgers) per bar guidance (State Bar of California resources/course: https://www.calbar.ca.gov/Attorneys/Conduct-Discipline/Client-Trust-Accounting-IOLTA/Client-Trust-Accounting-Resources). Disbursements should not be made against uncollected funds; many bars prohibit disbursing until funds are collected/cleared (Florida Bar FAQ: “In general, a lawyer cannot disburse until trust funds are collected.” https://www.floridabar.org/ethics/etfaq/frequently-asked-ethics-questions/; NC ethics opinions caution against disbursing against uncollected funds: https://www.ncbar.gov/for-lawyers/ethics/adopted-opinions/rpc-191/). Accounting systems are integrated with CMS to post settlement statements and entries (SmartAdvocate integrates with QuickBooks; disbursements in SmartAdvocate create bills in QBO: https://smartadvocate.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/KB/pages/1707737093/User+manual; QuickBooks integration spotlight: https://www.smartadvocate.com/spotlight-partner/quickbooks). Handoff: After liens and fees are finalized and funds cleared, the firm issues checks or e-payments to client and lienholders; transfers earned fees to operating.
- **common_tools**: ['CMS MSPRP portal', 'State Medicaid portals/agency contacts', 'CMS/Medicare vendor portals (e.g., Synergy, Massive/Epiq as service providers)', 'CMS + CMS-integrated lien tools (varies)', 'Case management settlement modules (SmartAdvocate/Filevine/CasePeer)', 'QuickBooks Online + trust accounting reports; LawPay for e-payments']
- **manual_steps**: ['Submitting settlement data and disputing unrelated charges with CMS/Medicaid', 'Negotiating provider balances and liens; obtaining reductions/compromises', 'Preparing settlement statements; securing client approvals', 'Three-way reconciliation and issuing checks once funds are collected']
- **pain_points**: ['Government lien timelines introduce hard waits; interest accrues if Medicare demands not timely paid (CMS).', 'State Medicaid processes vary and often require settlement details before issuing final amounts, delaying payouts (Epiq/Massive).', 'Ethics rules prohibit disbursing against uncollected funds; staff must wait for bank clearance, extending client wait times (Florida Bar/NC opinions).', 'High coordination overhead across multiple lienholders and client consent; rework when new bills arrive late.']
- **why_painful**: Lien finalization is dependency-bound and partially outside firm control. Trust accounting imposes strict sequencing: no early disbursement. Vendor integrations help with ledgering but not agency queue times.
- **business_impact**: Delays fee realization and client satisfaction; risk of interest penalties (Medicare) and bar discipline if mishandled. Heavy staff time in lien negotiations and reconciliations caps throughput.
